About LegacyFolio

What is LegacyFolio?

LegacyFolio is a digital legacy binder and end-of-life planner that helps you securely organize the information and documents your family and other trusted people may need to carry out your wishes and manage your affairs. It brings them together in a Folio: a password-protected PDF that you share with the people you trust.

Why is LegacyFolio different from a document, notes, or a spreadsheet?

A document or spreadsheet is blank. You have to decide what to include, and you won’t know what you’ve missed. LegacyFolio gives you categories with a guide to what each one can hold. It shows orange dots beside missing basic details, lists records that are missing basic information or are due for review, and can remind you to keep your records up to date.

You can easily add photos and PDF documents to each record. LegacyFolio contains a Directory of people, pets, places, and entities, so you enter each one once and link it to every record that needs it. Change a detail in one place and it’s right everywhere.

When you’re ready, LegacyFolio turns everything into a digital Folio, a password-protected PDF that the people you trust can open without the app. You can give your Folio to as many recipients as you like.

When things change, update your records and create a new Folio with the press of a button. Share it using your current Folio Password or a new one. Older Folios keep the Folio Password they were created with.

Do the people I share my Folio with need the app?

No. Your Folio recipients open your Folio in any regular web browser or PDF reader, using the password you give them.

Does LegacyFolio collect my personal information?

No. LegacyFolio stores your information on your devices and, if you choose to sync, in your personal Apple iCloud. We never access or store your information, photos, documents, or Folios.

Which devices does LegacyFolio work on?

LegacyFolio works on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. One universal license covers all three. If you use iCloud sync, your information stays current across all your Apple devices signed in to the same iCloud account.

Privacy and Security

Where is my information stored?

Your information stays on your devices and, if you choose to sync, in your personal Apple iCloud. LegacyFolio does not store any data on its own servers, and it never shares anything automatically.

Can anyone at LegacyFolio see my information?

No. We never access or store your information, photos, documents, or Folios. We also have no copy of your passwords, and we cannot recover them.

Is my information encrypted?

The following information is end-to-end encrypted in iCloud: identity numbers, such as passport, national ID, and health card numbers; account numbers; passwords, security questions, recovery codes, and seed phrases; access codes, key locations, and access instructions; dates of birth; your medical history, conditions, medications, and allergies; your Last Messages; and your Message to Reader. For everything else, including attachments, Apple holds the keys unless you turn on Apple’s Advanced Data Protection (ADP).

On a Mac, turn on FileVault to encrypt the data stored on the Mac itself. You can find it in System Settings > Privacy & Security > FileVault. Many newer Macs turn it on automatically.

Should I turn on Advanced Data Protection?

ADP applies end-to-end encryption to all of your iCloud data (not just LegacyFolio data), so that Apple cannot access it. It also means only you can recover your data. If you lose access to your devices and your recovery method, your data cannot be restored, not even by Apple. If you turn on ADP, set up a recovery key or recovery contact.

What protects LegacyFolio if someone else uses my device?

LegacyFolio has an App Lock to protect against unauthorized access. By default, App Lock is on and uses Device Unlock, which unlocks LegacyFolio the same way your device is unlocked (Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode; on a Mac, your login password). In Settings you can choose to use an App Password (a separate password for LegacyFolio) or No Lock (which means LegacyFolio never locks on any of your devices). You also choose how soon LegacyFolio asks you to unlock after you leave it. Your choice of lock syncs to your other devices through iCloud Keychain.

Is my Folio protected when I share it?

Yes. Every Folio is a password-protected PDF, so anyone who receives the file also needs the Folio Password to open it. For extra protection, share the Folio and its password by different methods.

Does address search send information anywhere?

When you search for an address, what you enter is sent to Apple Maps to find matches. Address search is optional. You can turn off address search in Settings.

Does LegacyFolio change my Contacts?

No. LegacyFolio can read the names, phone numbers and email addresses in your Contacts to check whether people you imported or linked have changed. It never changes them. If the details change in Contacts, LegacyFolio can alert you so you can choose which changes to accept. You can turn off these alerts by turning off Check Contacts for Changes in Settings.

Do backups of my device include LegacyFolio?

Yes. Backups of a device contain everything in LegacyFolio, including passwords and account numbers. Use encrypted backups.

Passwords

What passwords does LegacyFolio use?

  • App Password (optional): Protects LegacyFolio. By default, LegacyFolio is set to unlock the same way your device does. Choose an App Password in Settings if you want a separate password for LegacyFolio on all your devices.
  • Folio Password: Protects your Folio. You give it to the people who will read your Folio. To see your current Folio Password, print a Folio Password Card. A Folio Password you choose yourself must be at least 16 characters.
  • Archive Password: Protects your Archives (encrypted copies of your data).

Your passwords are stored in your iCloud Keychain, so they are available on every device signed in to your iCloud account.

What if I forget a password?

Password Warning: LegacyFolio cannot recover your passwords, and reinstalling the app will not reset your App Password. Choose passwords you will remember.

The App Password and the Archive Password can each have an optional hint, which LegacyFolio shows after two incorrect passwords. The Folio Password has no hint, because you can always see the current Folio Password in the Folio Password Card after you unlock LegacyFolio.

If you forget your App Password and Face ID or Touch ID still opens LegacyFolio on another of your devices, open it there and go to Settings > App Lock. You can change your App Password, or choose Device Unlock so there is no password to remember. If you cannot open LegacyFolio on any of your devices, you will have to erase all information and documents in LegacyFolio and start over. On the lock screen, choose Still Can’t Get In? Erase All Data…. This permanently deletes all of your LegacyFolio data (including your Archive Password and Folio Password) from all of your devices and from your iCloud, and it can’t be undone. Your license purchase is not affected. See Privacy, Security, and Sync in the User Guide.

If I change my Folio Password, will my older Folios still open?

Yes, but with the old password. A new Folio Password applies only to Folios created after the change. Each Folio opens with the password it was created with.

Your Folio

What is in my Folio?

  • A cover and a table of contents with clickable links.
  • Your Message to Reader, if you have written one.
  • Your Profile.
  • Your records, organized by category, with clickable links.
  • A Directory of the people, entities, pets, and places your records refer to, with clickable links.
  • Your attachments, all included at the end of the Folio, with an Attachments index that links to each file.
  • An End of Folio page.

Can I see how a record will look in my Folio?

Yes. Every record in LegacyFolio has a Preview Folio button that shows how the record content will appear in your Folio. You can hide the Preview Folio button in Settings. You can print a preview: on a Mac use File > Print… (⌘P); on iPhone and iPad use the Print button. A printed preview is not password-protected, so dispose of it carefully.

Can I review my Folio before I share it?

Yes. Creating a Folio does not share it. LegacyFolio never shares anything automatically. Create your Folio, open it with the Folio Password, and review it as often as you like. When you are happy with it, share it with the people you trust. If you find something to change, update your records and create a new Folio.

How do I share my Folio?

You decide when and how you share your Folio and its Folio Password. You can give someone your Folio and its Folio Password at the same time, or at different times, or by different methods. For example, you could send quarterly updates of your Folio to your agents or executors, and leave the Folio Password with your lawyer to share when the time comes.

Your Folio is a standard PDF file, so you can share it the same way you share any other file. LegacyFolio shows an estimated maximum size of your Folio before you create it, which can help you choose a method:

  • Email: Attach your Folio to an email. Most email services limit attachments to about 20–25 MB.
  • Mail Drop: For larger Folios, Apple Mail can send the file using Mail Drop, which uploads it to iCloud and emails a download link. Files up to 5 GB can be sent, and the link expires after 30 days, so ask your recipient to download and save the Folio.
  • AirDrop or Messages: Send your Folio directly to someone nearby with AirDrop, or in a message.
  • Cloud Storage: Upload your Folio to a service such as iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, or OneDrive, and share a link. Anyone with the link can download the Folio, but they still need the Folio Password to open it.
  • USB Drive: Save your Folio to a USB drive and give it to someone in person, or store it with your important documents.
  • Printed Copy: Print your Folio and give someone the paper copy. A printed Folio is not password-protected, so store it carefully. See Can I print my Folio? below.

For extra protection, give the Folio and its password by different methods. For example, email the Folio and give the password in person.

LegacyFolio can also print a Folio Password Card that shows the password and tells the reader how to open the Folio.

Can I print my Folio?

Yes. Open your Folio in any web browser or PDF reader, enter the Folio Password, and print it like any other document. When you create your Folio, you can choose a larger text size if it will be read on paper.

You can also give someone a printed Folio instead of an electronic copy, for example someone who prefers to read on paper.

Printing Warning: A printed Folio is not password-protected, so store and dispose of it as carefully as your other important documents.

How do I keep my Folio up to date?

Update your records whenever something changes, such as a new account, a new address, or a change to your will. Then create a new Folio and give it to the people you trust. You can use your current Folio Password or choose a new one.

LegacyFolio helps you keep your Folio current and complete. Orange dots show missing basic details. In the sidebar, under Tools, Missing Basic Information and Due for Review list the records that need attention. If a record is still correct, choose Still Correct. To hide a record for a while, choose Remind Me Later. LegacyFolio can also send you a notification, on a schedule you choose, to remind you to review your records.

Will my Folio still open if I stop using LegacyFolio?

Yes. Your Folio is a standard, password-protected PDF file. It opens in any web browser or PDF reader without the LegacyFolio app.

Do I still need to keep my original documents?

Yes. You can attach photos and PDFs of your documents, but your agents and executors may need the originals. Keep your original documents in safekeeping, and record the storage details in LegacyFolio.

Sync, Storage, and Archives

Can I use LegacyFolio without iCloud?

Yes. iCloud sync is optional. If you turn it off, all data stays on the device where you entered it and doesn’t appear on your other devices. So with iCloud sync off, you will have to use LegacyFolio on only one device.

If your data is not synced using iCloud, an Archive is your backup. Export an Archive regularly using the Archive controls in Settings, and keep the exported Archive somewhere safe, so you can restore your data if you lose your device or can no longer access the app.

What is an Archive?

An Archive is an encrypted copy of all your LegacyFolio data. An Archive is protected by an Archive Password, and only LegacyFolio can open it. Archive controls are in Settings. There you can also check that you still remember your Archive Password.

Archives are especially useful if you don’t use iCloud sync. Export an Archive to back up your data. Import an Archive to restore your data to LegacyFolio. Importing only adds missing records and fills in blank details. It never deletes or overwrites the data you already have. If you are using a free version of LegacyFolio, an Archive that would take you past 10 records is refused, and nothing is imported.

Does LegacyFolio use my iCloud storage?

Yes, if you use iCloud sync. Your data is stored in your own personal iCloud, so it counts against your Apple iCloud storage. LegacyFolio optimizes photos to save space.

What happens if I get a new iPhone, iPad, or Mac?

  • With iCloud Sync: Your data is stored in your personal Apple iCloud, so it is available on your new device when you sign in to the same iCloud account.
  • Without iCloud Sync: Export an Archive on your old device and import it on your new one.

What happens if I delete the app, lose my device, or can't access the app?

Data Warning: If iCloud sync is off, your data exists only on the device where you entered it. If you delete the app, lose the device, or can no longer access the app, your data cannot be recovered unless you have a previously exported Archive and its password. If iCloud sync is on and you lose your device or delete the app, your data remains in your iCloud. If you can no longer open LegacyFolio because you forgot your App Password, see “What if I forget a password?” above.

Can I recover a record I deleted?

The 30-Day Rule: A deleted record or Directory entry goes to Recently Deleted for 30 days, where you can restore it. After 30 days it is automatically, permanently deleted. Deleting an attachment is immediate and permanent.

Pricing & Limits

What are the limits of the free and paid licenses?

  • Free License: The free version lets you have up to 10 records, and your Directory, My Profile, and attachments are not counted. Nothing else is limited, including Folio creation. At the ten-record limit, LegacyFolio offers the purchase screen instead of creating a record. Records in Recently Deleted count toward the limit until they are permanently deleted. In Settings, under License, you can see how many of your 10 records you have used.
  • Paid License: A paid license removes the record limit. There is no annual fee. It is a one-time purchase.
  • iCloud Storage & Sync: iCloud storage and sync capacity is determined by your personal Apple iCloud subscription and available space.

One universal license covers your iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

How do I restore my purchase?

If you have already purchased LegacyFolio, you can choose Restore Purchases in Settings, under License. It restores the purchase made with the Apple Account on that device.
