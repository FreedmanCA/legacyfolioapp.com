1. Welcome to LegacyFolio

LegacyFolio is a private app for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. It helps you collect the information, photos, and documents that your executor and substitute decision-makers will need, and brings them together in a Folio: a well-organized, password-protected PDF that you share with the people you trust.

You fill in LegacyFolio during your lifetime. The people who receive your Folio never need the app. They open the PDF in any regular web browser or PDF reader, using the password you give them.

LegacyFolio collects no data and requires no account. It has no cloud of its own. Your information stays on your devices and, if you choose to sync, in your private Apple iCloud.

2. Getting Started

  • App Password: LegacyFolio asks you to set an App Password that locks the app. It must be at least 8 characters. It is stored in your iCloud Keychain and protects LegacyFolio on every device signed in to your iCloud account, so removing it leaves LegacyFolio unprotected everywhere. You can also turn on Face ID or Touch ID, which asks for your App Password first. If you forget your App Password, LegacyFolio cannot recover it, and reinstalling the app will not reset it, so choose one you will remember.
  • Privacy and security notice: The first time you use LegacyFolio, a short notice explains what is stored, what is encrypted, and what is sent to Apple.
  • The Essentials: The Essentials are a personalized list of some of the most important records, at the top of the sidebar. A few quick questions when you first start help you begin with them.
  • Saving: There is no Save button. LegacyFolio saves your changes as you make them.

3. Your Profile and Message to Reader

My Profile

My Profile holds your own details. It is the starting point for your Folio.

Message to Reader

Message to Reader is a personal message from you, and it is the first page your Folio opens with. Write it in your own words, and change it whenever you like. If you leave it empty, your Folio has no message page.

4. Adding Your Records

Your records are organized into eight categories, listed in the sidebar:

  • Identity & Official Documents: passports and IDs, vital records, immigration and visas, professional credentials, licenses and permits, and military service.
  • Dependents & Pets: minor children, dependent adults, pet care, and other care arrangements.
  • Legal Records: your will, power of attorney, trusts, and family law documents.
  • Health & Medical: medical directives, health insurance, doctors and care team, conditions, medications and allergies, housing and personal care, and organ donation.
  • Finances: bank accounts, credit cards, investments and retirement, income and benefits, insurance, debts, taxes, and more.
  • Property & Business Interests: real estate, vehicles, business interests, personal property, secure storage, and property services.
  • Digital Devices & Accounts: devices, password managers, email, cloud storage, subscriptions, and websites.
  • Wishes & Legacy: funeral arrangements, notifications, charity, last messages, and sensitive personal items.

Not sure where something goes?

Each category has a guide, shown with the information (ⓘ) button, that lists the kinds of records each section can hold.

Missing information

LegacyFolio knows which details your executor or substitute decision-maker would need in order to act. A small orange dot beside a field means it has not been filled in yet. You can turn these dots off in Settings.

Photos and PDFs

You can attach photos and PDF documents to your records. Photos are optimized to save space, and PDFs stay PDFs. Deleting an attachment is permanent, so LegacyFolio asks you first.

5. The Directory: Entities, People, Pets, and Places

The Directory, below the categories in the sidebar, holds Entities (such as a law firm, a bank, or another organization), People, Pets, and Places. Enter each one once, and link it to any record that needs it. You do not have to retype your lawyer’s details for every record.

Importing from Contacts

  • Import: Import people from your Contacts. LegacyFolio only reads Contacts. It never changes them.
  • Stay current: If a contact’s details change, LegacyFolio shows the old and new details side by side, and you choose which changes to accept.
  • Turning it off: A switch in Settings turns Contacts sync off.

6. Keeping Your Information Current

Your information changes, and your Folio is only as useful as it is current. LegacyFolio helps in two ways.

Records to Review

  • Out of Date: Lists records you have not updated for 6 months, 1 year, or 2 years. You choose the period.
  • Missing Basic Information: Lists records that are missing details an executor or substitute decision-maker would need.
  • Remind Me Later: Swipe a record, or use its menu, to set it aside for 3, 6, or 12 months without changing it.

Review reminders

LegacyFolio can send you a notification on a schedule you choose, to remind you to review your information. This reminder does not check your records. It simply prompts you to look.

7. Creating Your Folio

Choose Create Folio in the sidebar when you are ready.

  • Folio Password: Your Folio is protected by a password. Let LegacyFolio generate a random password, or type your own. A password you type must be at least 12 characters, using standard letters, numbers, and symbols, with no accented characters. If LegacyFolio generates it, it shows you the password and asks you to confirm that you have recorded it. Your Folio Password is stored in your iCloud Keychain, and you can see it again whenever you print a Folio Password Card.
  • Text size: Choose how large the text in your Folio should be.
  • Size: LegacyFolio shows the upper size of the Folio before it is created.
  • Password Card: Print a Folio Password Card. It shows the password and tells the reader how to open the Folio.

What is in your Folio

  • A cover and a table of contents with clickable links.
  • Your Message to Reader, if you have written one.
  • Your records, organized by category, with numbered pages.
  • Your attachments. PDFs are included as real pages, and each photo has its own page.
  • A record with no details says so, so a reader can see that the record exists.

Preview Folio

Every record has a Preview Folio button that shows how that record will appear in your Folio. You can hide this button in Settings.

8. Sharing Your Folio and Password

You decide when and how to share your Folio and its password, together or separately.

  • Together or separately: Give someone the Folio and the password at the same time, or at different times, or by different routes.
  • Example: Send your executor updated copies of your Folio, and leave the password with your lawyer to share when the time comes.
  • Updating: Create a new Folio whenever your information changes, and give your trusted people the new file.
  • Changing the password: You can change your Folio Password at any time. A Folio you have already shared still opens with the password it was created with, so a password card you printed earlier will still match it.

9. Backing Up

The best way to back up LegacyFolio is to use iCloud sync. When you are signed in to iCloud, your information is kept in your private Apple iCloud as well as on your devices, and it is there if you replace a device or delete the app.

  • Recommended: iCloud sync. Leave iCloud sync on. It backs up your information automatically, with nothing to remember. Sync is controlled in your device’s Settings.
  • If you do not use iCloud sync: Your information exists only on the device where you entered it, so make an Archive regularly. If you delete the app or lose the device without an Archive, your information cannot be recovered.

Archives

An Archive is an encrypted copy of everything in LegacyFolio. It is meant for people who do not use iCloud sync. It is different from a Folio: a Folio goes to someone else to read, and an Archive comes back to you.

  • Archive Password: An Archive is protected by its own password, at least 12 characters. It is separate from your App Password and your Folio Password. Keep it somewhere safe and never share it. It is stored in your iCloud Keychain, so your other devices can make and restore Archives without asking for it.
  • Only LegacyFolio opens it: An Archive can only be restored into LegacyFolio. Your executor should use your Folio.
  • Restoring adds, never replaces: Restoring an Archive adds records that are missing. It never deletes or overwrites what you have.
  • Reminders: LegacyFolio can remind you to make a new Archive.
  • Opening a file: Opening an Archive from Files, Mail, or Finder does not restore it. Use Restore in Settings.

10. Privacy, Security, and Sync

LegacyFolio does not store your information on its own servers, and it never shares anything automatically. Your information stays on your devices and, if you choose to sync, in your private Apple iCloud.

  • iCloud sync: LegacyFolio can sync across all your Apple devices signed in to the same iCloud account, so your information is current on your Mac, iPad, and iPhone. If you would rather not store any data with Apple, turn off iCloud sync for LegacyFolio in your device’s iCloud settings. Your information then stays on that device only, so keep your own copies, such as a current Folio or Archive.
  • Encryption: ID and account numbers, access and recovery codes, seed phrases, medical details, legacy letters, and your Message to Reader are always end-to-end encrypted in iCloud. For everything else, Apple holds the keys unless you turn on Apple’s Advanced Data Protection (ADP).
  • Advanced Data Protection (ADP): You can turn on ADP in your device settings to apply end-to-end encryption to all of your iCloud data, so that Apple cannot access it. If you turn it on, only you can recover your data. If you lose access to your devices and your recovery method, your data cannot be restored, not even by Apple. Set up a recovery key or recovery contact when you turn it on.
  • On a Mac: iCloud settings protect your data in Apple’s iCloud, but not the data stored on your Mac. FileVault encrypts the data on your Mac. You can turn it on in System Settings > Privacy & Security > FileVault. Many newer Macs turn it on automatically.
  • Your passwords: LegacyFolio uses three passwords: the App Password, the Archive Password, and the Folio Password. All three are stored in your iCloud Keychain, so they are available on every device signed in to your iCloud account.
  • Address search: When you search for an address, what you type is sent to Apple Maps to find matches.
  • Apple Contacts: LegacyFolio reads Contacts for people you import or link, so it can offer to update their information. It never changes anything in your Contacts. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Original documents: You can attach PDF documents, scans, and photos to each record. Legal requirements may mean your substitute decision-makers and executors need the original documents. Keep your originals in safekeeping, and record where they are stored in LegacyFolio.

11. Deleting and Restoring

  • Recently Deleted: A record you delete goes to Recently Deleted for 30 days. You can restore it during that time. After 30 days it is permanently deleted.
  • Attachments: Deleting an attachment is immediate and permanent. LegacyFolio asks you to confirm first.

12. Settings

Settings is where you control how LegacyFolio works. Each setting is labeled in the app, and these are the main groups.

  • General: Show or hide Preview Folio buttons, The Essentials, and the information (ⓘ) icons that give help and tips.
  • Records to Review: Choose whether to flag out-of-date records and missing information, how long a record can go without an update, and how long Remind Me Later lasts. These settings apply on all of your devices.
  • Review notification: Turn on a reminder to review LegacyFolio every 3, 6, or 12 months.
  • App Password & Biometrics: Set, change, or remove your App Password, turn on Face ID or Touch ID, and choose how soon LegacyFolio locks after you leave it.
  • iCloud Sync: See whether sync is active, and read how your data is protected.
  • Contacts: Turn on or off the checking of imported people for changes.
  • Export & Import: Make or restore an Archive, set the Archive Password, and choose Archive reminders. See Backing Up.
  • Support, Legal, and About: Open this guide, contact support, and read the Privacy Policy and license agreement.
